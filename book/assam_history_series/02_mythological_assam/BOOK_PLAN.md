# Mythological Assam: Danava, Naraka, and the Sacred Worlds of Pragjyotisha

**Series position:** Book 02 of *Assam: A History of Its Land and People*.

**Coverage:** Mythic narrative time, textual transmission, and later regional reception; no asserted BCE/CE reign dates.

**Status:** Plan only; manuscript drafting has not begun.

## Scope, names, and relationship to the set

This is a dedicated treatment of stories, religious meaning, textual transmission, and regional memory. Its position after prehistory is a reading arrangement, **not a claim that a dated mythological age lies between archaeology and the Varmans**. Preserve Sura Danava as a named research question until a source establishes its identification.

The former combined Kamarupa plan is retained as a [superseded planning snapshot](../_superseded/02_ancient_kamarupa/README.md); use Books 03–05 as the active dynasty plans. Historical material about sacred ancestry belongs in both the mythology book and dynasty-specific analysis, with explicit cross-references rather than forced continuity.

## Audience, scale, and AxomRank identity

Write for serious general readers, students, and researchers in accessible English, preserving accurate local names and original-language forms. A commissioned Assamese edition requires full translation and fluent editorial review. Follow the [shared AxomRank editorial and visual system](../EDITORIAL_AND_BRAND_SYSTEM.md).

Working main-text allocation: **40,000–60,000 words**, excluding notes and appendices. This is a flexible estimate, not a measured manuscript or a quota for sparse evidence. The three Kamarupa books receive their own period-specific institutions, society, landscape, and material culture, rather than a list of kings with generic repeated background.

- **Ghataka and the Naraka narratives:** Chapters 5–8.
