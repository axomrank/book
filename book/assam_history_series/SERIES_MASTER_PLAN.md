# Assam: A History of Its Land and People

**Proposed AxomRank umbrella series.** Revised 11 October 2026. Thirteen books, 222 chapters, and 1,110 numbered content divisions. These are editorial plans, not newly written or fully verified historical manuscripts. The selected title **From Sukaphaa to Gomdhar Konwar** remains the title of the three existing Ahom volumes.

## Scope and promise

Build a connected, source-documented account from the earliest recoverable human evidence to the contemporary state. Follow people, landscapes, institutions, political power, livelihoods, languages, religious traditions, cultural work, and everyday experience. Explain historical change and disagreement in original prose. Each book should be independently understandable, while precise cross-references connect the set.

“Assam” is a changing historical subject. Current state borders cannot define every earlier polity, excavation region, colonial province, or post-independence jurisdiction. The Brahmaputra Valley, Barak Valley, present hill districts, and historically connected areas receive period-specific treatment. Neighbouring regions appear where evidence and relationships require them; their histories are not absorbed into Assam merely because they once shared an administrative structure.

Prehistoric dates depend on actual sites and methods. Modern ethnicity, language, genetic ancestry, and a prehistoric artifact assemblage are different categories. Later royal or epic traditions must be identified as traditions rather than turned into a verified prehistoric ruler list. Early political dates and capital identifications remain working questions until exact witnesses are compared.

The divisions are useful editorial boundaries, not claims of identical social transitions across every region. Each book must explain its overlap with its neighbours and avoid retelling whole campaigns or biographies when a precise reference suffices.

## Ownership of shared subjects

- Book 01 owns archaeological method and prehistoric site dossiers. Books 03–05 own period-specific historical inscription and ruler concordances, sharing acquisition records for sites such as Ambari where relevant.
- Book 02 owns extended myth retellings and comparison of textual variants. Books 03–05 own the actual historical dynasties' use of sacred lineage claims. Preserve Sura Danava as a source-identification task until a witness resolves it; do not fabricate a biography or silently replace the name. The mythology book is not a dated historical interval.
- Use Barma/Barman/Varman, Salstambha/Salastambha, and Pal/Pala as recorded naming variants, with a consistent authority guide. Bhauma/Naraka lineage traditions receive treatment across mythology and all three dynasties, not an exclusive synonym for Salastambha. The [consulted genealogy study](https://list.indology.info/pipermail/indology/attachments/20170209/d815b926/attachment.pdf) informs this distinction; verify original inscription passages separately.
- Book 06 owns sustained non-Ahom political and community narratives. Books 07–09 retain detailed Ahom reigns and campaigns. Shared events receive different perspectives, with explicit chapter links and source-lineage comparison rather than interchangeable accounts.
- Book 09 retains full Ahom institutional history, Moamoria narrative, Burmese occupation, and Gomdhar’s 1828 dossier. Book 10 gives the colonial transition a short bridge and develops 1830 resistance and later restoration separately. Original Ahom scope remains unchanged.
- Book 10 owns plantation origins, nineteenth-century extraction, and initial language/print changes. Books 11–13 explain subsequent change without repeating origin chapters at full length.
- Book 11 owns Sylhet and the 1947 boundary transition; Book 12 follows subsequent administrative and household consequences. A community’s prior history is not reduced to its Partition experience.
- Book 12 owns the history of reorganisation and the signed Accord; Book 13 owns later implementation, subsequent settlements, and contemporary institutions. A statute, an agreement, a court ruling, and its lived effects require distinct evidence.
- Shared cross-period topics receive concise background followed by period-specific analysis. Do not project an eighteenth-century institution or modern census category unchanged across the whole series.

## Readers, languages, and proposed scale

Write for serious general readers, students, and researchers. Start in accessible English with accurate local names, transliterations, and glossaries. A full Assamese edition can share the evidence database, but needs complete translation of explanation and notes and fluent editorial/typesetting review. Other language editions may be commissioned later; they are not created by this plan.

The ten non-Ahom books have a combined provisional main-text allocation of 450,000–655,000 words. The existing Ahom trilogy retains its shared 220,000–300,000-word target. The whole set therefore has a provisional main-text range of **670,000–955,000 words**, excluding notes and appendices. These are flexible editorial recommendations, not finished word counts. Sparse evidence must not be padded to reach a target. Adjust allocations after dossiers and sample chapters demonstrate the required space.

Use the existing 7 × 10-inch portrait format as a series design starting point. Final trim, paper, colour treatment, binding, type scale, and pagination follow actual manuscript, imagery, budget, and printer requirements. Do not promise a page count or completion date without those inputs.

## Chapter dossier and writing pattern

Every planned chapter contains five distinct content divisions and a proposed supporting visual. Those divisions define research coverage rather than a formula for five equally sized sections. Develop a dossier with:

1. Stable chapter ID, coverage dates, central question, and relation to adjacent chapters.
2. All five divisions mapped to evidence, named actors, places, and unresolved issues.
3. Sources actually read, exact edition/translation, pages or folios, PDF page when relevant, and source dependencies.
4. A chronology and name/place authority check, including competing readings and their rationale.
5. Regional and social perspective check: whose experience survives and whose is absent in the records.
6. Proposed maps, objects, images, tables, quotations, captions, credits, and rights status.
7. Missing sources, acquisition attempts, specialist questions, and honest draft status.

Open with a documented event, object, place, or question. Explain context, change, causes, consequences, and alternatives. Give notes, a brief chronology or orientation where useful, an annotated further-reading list, and an ending that connects to the next historical problem. Keep development notes outside reader-facing prose. Never invent dialogue, private thoughts, numbers, dates, bibliographic details, or a seamless biography to cover an evidence gap.

## Evidence and research standards

The [source starter register](SOURCE_STARTER_REGISTER.md) contains limited inspected summaries and acquisition routes. It is not a bibliography of works fully read for the new books. Obtain exact excavation reports, inscription editions, chronicle witnesses, treaty and legislative texts, archival files, newspapers, statistical tables, and relevant scholarship before presenting their claims as findings.

For every source, record identifier, author/editor, title, language, source type, composition and edition/copy dates, translation, repository or URL, shelfmark, consulted passages, access date, dependencies, and rights. For every substantive claim, record precise statement, date/place, people, earliest located witness, supporting source lineages, conflicting evidence, exact locator, interpretation, assigned chapter, and verification status. Quotes need exact text and attribution; an indirect citation stays indirect.

Official records are evidence of recorded actions and institutions, with their own perspective and omissions. Government summaries may contain errors and are not universal historical authority. Compare political, administrative, court-centred, employer, and participant accounts with independent evidence where available. Gait and Barua are partly related witnesses; their agreement does not independently confirm an event.

Treat oral histories with attribution, date, context, informed consent, and limits. Community histories and religious memory can preserve meaningful perspectives without becoming automatically contemporary testimony. Compare translations and refer uncertain original-language readings to relevant expertise.

Contemporary disputes require particular precision: distinguish allegation, inquiry finding, judgment, conviction, appeal, and unresolved responsibility. Do not infer anyone’s citizenship from language, religion, surname, or community. Date legal and institutional descriptions and verify the applicable version before drafting; this plan supplies historical research topics rather than legal advice.

## Geography, numbers, and the present-day cutoff

Each map needs period, jurisdiction, scale, sources, uncertain boundaries, and a caption distinguishing claimed from controlled territory. Modern administrative maps may orient a reader but must be labelled as modern context. Cross-period statistical comparison requires geographic concordances and methodology notes; where adjustment is impossible, present separate series rather than a misleading trend.

For contemporary data, record publication date, data reference period, provisional/revised status, units, price base if relevant, denominator, and territory. A census table dated 2011 does not measure the population in 2026. Verify the latest accessible released evidence rather than silently updating old figures. Record gaps and publication lag.

Book 13’s edition closes on **11 October 2026, Asia/Kolkata**. Maintain a dated event register up to that point. A policy announcement is not enactment; enactment is not implementation; implementation is not measured success. Events and revisions after the cutoff belong in a correction note or a subsequent edition, with substantive changes tracked. Do not predict future events as history.

Develop book-specific appendices in their own folders and a series-wide authority catalogue connecting people, places, institutions, events, sources, objects, and historical names. Preserve all existing Ahom IDs. New chapter IDs use `B01-C01` through their book-specific ranges; new evidence IDs should be book-prefixed and stable. Books 07–09 retain original Ahom chapter numbers even though they occupy different positions in the umbrella series.

The [planning ID migration ledger](PLANNING_ID_MIGRATION.json) records renumbered series planning IDs and the one-to-many expansion of the former combined Kamarupa outline. It does not rename Ahom manuscript chapters or evidence IDs. The [superseded combined plan](_superseded/02_ancient_kamarupa/README.md) remains a provenance snapshot; Books 03–05 are the active historical plans.

Provide a combined chronology, historical boundary atlas, names and transliteration guide, source-lineage diagram, topical index, place index, and cross-book concordance once actual content exists. Every important record receives a destination in a chapter, appendix, or unresolved-claims register. An empty catalogue is not completed research.

## Delivery sequence

1. Use this plan as the initial scope and acquire the foundational corpora for each era. Record source gaps rather than assuming internet access supplies a complete archive.
2. Build chronology and authority records and develop chapter dossiers. Continue the existing Ahom acquisition work without creating a competing manuscript.
3. Pilot one evidence-rich chapter per new book to test voice, chapter depth, notes, and realistic word allocation.
4. Draft in manageable book batches after adequate source comparison, with chapter-specific priorities from each `RESEARCH_PLAN.md`.
5. Integrate cross-book references and regional coverage; check that thematic repetition explains historical change.
6. Obtain historical and relevant language/specialist review, resolve high-impact disputes where possible, and retain remaining uncertainty visibly.
7. Produce maps, genealogies, illustrations, credits, bibliography, and indexes; clear publication rights.
8. Render and inspect proofs, complete copyediting and typesetting review, and update actual progress and corrections records.

The plans do not schedule automatic drafting, create a release deadline, or publish books. GitHub publication follows the user’s actual instruction.

## Completion criteria

- All 222 chapters have substantive dossiers and delivered text or an explicit, documented destination after a scope revision.
- All 1,110 content divisions have evidence-backed coverage or a stated inability to establish the proposed subject.
- Dates, translated terms, person identities, numerical claims, and maps have locatable sources and recorded disputes.
- Barak Valley, hill districts, diverse communities, women, labour, religion, language, and environmental history receive actual treatment appropriate to each era.
- Contemporary facts are fixed to a transparent source cutoff and reference period.
- Existing Ahom chapter IDs, selected titles, drafts, and evidence provenance remain intact.
- Each book is edited and readable independently, with consistent AxomRank branding, checked cross-references, notes, bibliography, indexes, permissions, and inspected final proofs.
