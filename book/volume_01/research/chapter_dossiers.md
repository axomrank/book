# Volume I — Chapter research dossiers

Updated 10 October 2026. Scope: Chapters 1–16, through the working endpoint of 1603. All dossiers are preliminary. Chapters 1–5 have opening prose drafts; Chapters 6–16 have research briefs, not finished prose.

A source listed below is actually consulted only to the extent recorded in the source register. Gait and Barua partly share evidence. Additional titles are acquisition tasks, not citations to unread contents.

## Chapter 01 — How We Know Ahom History

**Period:** Method and sources. **Status:** opening prose draft, v0.1.

### Agreed content coverage

1. Buranjis in Tai-Ahom and Assamese: authorship, manuscript families, copying, translation, and court purposes.
2. Inscriptions, copper plates, coins, archaeology, diplomatic letters, religious literature, genealogies, and material culture.
3. Persian, Burmese, British, and other external accounts: what each can and cannot establish.
4. Colonial, nationalist, regional, community, and recent academic interpretations; missing voices and archival silences.
5. Rules for separating documented events, probable reconstructions, oral traditions, legends, and modern commemorative claims.

**Consulted baseline:** G1906 introduction pp. i–vii; B1930 preface PDF 3; DHAS and EAP catalogue leads.

**Initial findings:** The first source pass establishes the Barua–Gait dependency and editorial intervention.

**Outstanding research:** Compare independent buranji families; manuscript provenance; modern historiography; evidence for voices absent from court narratives.

**Planned visual:** Source-family diagram and a sample comparison of conflicting accounts. This is a future research commission, not a completed verified illustration.

## Chapter 02 — The Brahmaputra Valley on the Eve of 1228

**Period:** Background to 1228. **Status:** opening prose draft, v0.1.

### Agreed content coverage

1. River systems, floodplains, forests, hill passages, wet-rice zones, and travel constraints.
2. Political landscape after the fragmentation of Kamarupa; distinguish later state boundaries from medieval ones.
3. Moran, Borahi, Chutiya, Kachari/Dimasa, Bhuyan, and other communities: their own histories and changing relationships.
4. Existing settlement, cultivation, exchange, religion, and languages before Ahom arrival.
5. How geography shaped the possibilities for state formation, warfare, and diplomacy.

**Consulted baseline:** G1906 pp. 34–46, 75–76; B1930 pp. 45–47; S2018 section 3.2.

**Initial findings:** A plural political setting and the importance of floods emerge; exact 1228 boundaries are not established.

**Outstanding research:** Pre-Ahom archaeology and inscriptions; community histories; river changes; agricultural chronology; dated use of Kamata/Kamarupa.

**Planned visual:** A background map with approximate political zones and a river-and-route map. This is a future research commission, not a completed verified illustration.

## Chapter 07 — Succession Crises and Rule without a King

**Period:** 1332–1397; working chronology. **Status:** research dossier; prose pending.

### Agreed content coverage

1. Sukhrangphaa, Sutuphaa, and Tyao Khamti: separate biographies and reign narratives.
2. Political roles of nobles, royal households, and competing claimants.
3. Interregna: who exercised authority, why a ruler was not installed, and what sources say.
4. Assassination traditions, external encounters, and chronology disagreements.
5. Institutional continuity despite disputed succession; avoid assuming the state ceased to exist during an interregnum.

**Consulted baseline:** G1906 pp. 78–80; B1930 pp. 48–49.

**Initial findings:** The most urgent chronological conflict: Gait moves directly from Sukhrangphaa in 1364 to Sutuphaa; Barua records a five-year vacancy but also a contradictory reign duration. Later interregna and Tyao Khamti date problems must be retained.

**Outstanding research:** Calendar/edition comparison; vacancy 1364–1369; printed Tyao Khamti death 1380; assassination variants; authority exercised by nobles during vacancies.

**Planned visual:** A king-and-interregnum timeline with competing date columns. This is a future research commission, not a completed verified illustration.

## Chapter 16 — Diplomacy and the Regional Order before 1603

**Period:** Thirteenth–sixteenth centuries; synthesis. **Status:** research dossier; prose pending.

### Agreed content coverage

1. Comparative relations with Kamata/Koch, Chutiya, Kachari/Dimasa, Bhuyans, and eastern Tai polities.
2. Envoys, marriage, gifts, tribute, hostage arrangements, written agreements, and ceremonial ranking.
3. Markets, passes, buffer zones, and the difference between tribute and direct administration.
4. Periodic conflict and negotiation with hill and frontier communities; terminology must be historically explained.
5. A source-backed register of every recoverable diplomatic mission before 1603.

**Consulted baseline:** G1906 pp. 76–101; B1930 pp. 25, 44–50 consulted as preliminary witnesses.

**Initial findings:** A synthesis must distinguish kinship, gifts, marriage, tribute, sanctuary, hostages and direct administration. The mission register is only seeded; it is not yet a list of every recoverable mission.

**Outstanding research:** Extract all missions from further editions; identify sender/recipient; date and place; translation of treaty/oath terms; external confirmation; exclude later missions from pre-1603 register.

**Planned visual:** A diplomatic-network map and mission register. This is a future research commission, not a completed verified illustration.
