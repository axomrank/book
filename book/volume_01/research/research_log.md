# Volume I — Research log

**Date:** 10 October 2026. **Scope:** origins and Chapters 1–16, ending at the working date 1603.

## Work performed

- Retrieved complete scans of Gait’s 1906 *A History of Assam* and Barua’s 1930 bilingual *Ahom-Buranji*. Recorded byte sizes and SHA-256 checksums in the download inventory and source register.
- Extracted Gait using `pdftotext -layout`; read the introduction, relevant background, and printed pp. 67–102 for the early Ahom narrative. OCR remains a reading aid; spelling, table structure, and numerals can be damaged.

Rendered and visually inspected selected pages, including the preface, genealogical passages, founder-related service traditions, migration and early regnal sequence. Only the English translation was read; no new translation from Tai-Ahom was produced.
- Consulted the official DHAS publication list and British Library EAP collection/item metadata to identify further editions and manuscripts. This is catalogue research, not a reading of all listed works.
- Located SOAS thesis metadata and modern scholarship on state formation and agriculture. Full texts not obtained are marked explicitly in the source register.
- Read the historical rice discussion in section 3.2 of Saud’s IIT Guwahati thesis through the institutional PDF. Used only a narrow uncertainty statement in the manuscript, not its entire historical interpretation.
- Checked the official UNESCO Charaideo property description for modern site orientation. No individual mound attribution was inferred from it.
- Created opening prose for Chapters 1–5; first-pass dossiers for Chapters 1–16; 69 initial claim records; a comparative regnal chronology; and 11 seed diplomatic episode records.

## Acquisition limitations

The Acharyya 1966 mirror returned HTTP 403. The older SOAS thesis PDF URL could not be resolved; its current repository metadata was located separately. An alternative Barua OCR download timed out. A former RGU teaching-material PDF link returned 404. These outcomes are recorded in the JSON inventories and do not establish that the works are unavailable elsewhere.

No substantive argument has been attributed to an unread full text. Located titles and abstracts remain research leads. Further acquisition should use official repositories, publishers, libraries, or lawful digitised editions.

## Evidence decisions

1. Gait and Barua are partly dependent witnesses. Their agreement is not automatically independent corroboration.
2. Reported numbers, genealogies, ritual descriptions, and violence remain identified as source reports or traditions. Precision is not treated as proof.
3. Printed dates that conflict with a source’s own durations are retained as conflicts. No silent correction has been applied.
4. Colonial racial and civilisational rankings are not used as explanations of community history.
5. Modern communities and borders are not projected unchanged into the medieval period.
6. No invented dialogue, private thoughts, battle scenes, map coordinates, object accession numbers, or population totals have been added.
7. No gallery image has been used as historical evidence. Any later AI illustration must be labelled as a modern interpretive illustration, with its source and rights checked.

## Reproducibility

Downloaded sources remain under `research/sources/`; extraction outputs under `research/extracted/`; selected rendered pages under `research/page_checks/`. Bibliographic details, access levels, and hashes are in `source_register.json`. Claim IDs are stable within this initial release. Preserve them when adding or correcting records.

The manuscript is original English synthesis. Modern copyrighted sources are used narrowly and paraphrased; the bibliography is not a claim to have read every item in full. A publication edition still needs specialist review, wider corroboration, copyediting, illustrations, and a complete bibliography and index.
